# When Can Open Decision Models Replace Generative LLM Calls?

Ju Zhang (jz3702) · Weiyu Chen (wc2973) · Yizhi Hu (yh4060)

**Research question and contribution.** How reliably can frozen decision models handle intent routing and evidence checks, and when does confidence-based fallback improve the accuracy-efficiency tradeoff? Building on JevCascade [7] and prevalence-shift research [8], we independently evaluate released systems, task-specific recalibration, and complete decision-to-LLM pipelines. This field is moving rapidly; our contribution is reproducible evidence about useful operating conditions and failure cases, rather than a new architecture.

**Data and acquisition.** We use official human-labeled CLINC150 [1] and SQuAD 2.0 [2] downloads. CLINC comprises 15 banking intents plus out-of-scope (OOS): 400 calibration, 400 validation, and 600 test examples (450 banking + 150 OOS), maintaining 25% OOS; 850 additional OOS examples support a prevalence-shift stress test. SQuAD measures given-passage answerability: 1,000 calibration and 1,000 validation examples from article-disjoint training subsets, plus 2,000 balanced, locked examples from official dev. Sampling manifests, deduplication, and separate prompt-development data prevent project-level leakage.

**Methods and limitations.** We compare frozen Kev-4B v1.0 [3], StartLux-Decision-v1.1-4B-Instant [4], and Qwen3.5-4B [5] with thinking disabled and constrained JSON. Adapters provide equivalent task information and labels through documented interfaces. For decision models, we compare released probabilities with task-specific temperature scaling [6], fitted only on calibration data. Direct decisions, always-Qwen, and low-confidence fallback use actual predictions; Qwen is not assumed superior. CLINC informed StartLux development and a Kev temperature-retention comparison; its influence on pinned Kev weight selection is unconfirmed. Upstream exposure, including Qwen's, remains incompletely known. These are system comparisons, not isolated architecture effects.

**Evaluation and ablations.** For each workload, the primary endpoint is test local coverage at a threshold maximizing validation coverage subject to at most 5% empirical local error and at least 50 accepted cases; no feasible validation threshold means zero coverage. We report actual test risk with 95% intervals and flag target misses, without claiming a risk guarantee. We measure cascade accuracy, Brier/ECE, live end-to-end p50/p95 latency, throughput, and GPU-seconds per 1,000 decisions on matched hardware. Controls include released versus refitted temperatures, direct versus gated decisions, serial versus supported batching, and random routing and a gain-based hindsight oracle at matched deferral rates. Paired bootstrap uses article clusters for SQuAD. Probability calibration and ranking are distinguished. An optional StartLux-4B extension compares explicit OOS with none-based OOS detection, separately from fallback.

**Execution and deliverables.** We plan three parallel roles: one member leads CLINC experiments, one SQuAD experiments, and one runtime correctness and end-to-end performance. Individual assignments remain to be agreed; all use decision models, with Qwen as a shared baseline. Week 1 establishes shared splits/adapters, named configuration maintainers, and small-sample cross-runs; a day-5 review selects a verified runtime path. Week 2 completes baselines; weeks 3-4 cover calibration, routing, and ablations; remaining time covers verification and presentation. Each member delivers tests, analysis, and documentation. We release the evaluation harness, manifests, versioned configurations, and a reproducible notebook/CLI demo. Scope is single-GPU inference and scalar calibration, with no weight fine-tuning or required UI.
